About the project

How would an astronaut survive a severe limb bleed in space?

Who we are

Team Stasis is a University of Toronto student team competing in CAN-RGX 10, the Canadian Reduced Gravity Experiment Design Challenge. We design, build and fly an experiment on a parabolic flight, supervised by our faculty advisor, Prof. Fatemeh Jazinizadeh.

The problem

A tourniquet stops arterial bleeding, but left on for more than about two hours it can cause nerve injury and permanent tissue damage. Evacuating an injured astronaut takes far longer than that. Hemostatic gels can seal a wound so the tourniquet can come off, but no one has tested how they behave without gravity.

Our experiment

We simulate a femoral artery laceration in a closed-loop blood-simulant circuit. During each microgravity window a tourniquet valve is released and we measure how much fluid escapes past a VETIGEL seal, compared with identical runs on the ground at 1 g.

The science

Three questions we are flying to answer

I.

Does the gel stop the bleeding?

Without gravity, blood leaving an arterial wound pools over it as a dome or breaks into floating droplets. We measure how much VETIGEL cuts the leak from a simulated femoral laceration, in microgravity and at 1 g.

Read more in the proposal
II.

Does the seal hold in space?

As the tourniquet valve releases from 0 to 100 mmHg, we film the gel plug and track whether it stays put, lifts at the edges or washes out, and compare that with the same test on Earth.

Read more in the proposal
III.

Can it be applied in flight?

A remotely triggered syringe injects gel into a wound that is already bleeding. We test whether it can push through the floating dome of blood and cover the wound without gravity to help it settle.

Read more in the proposal
Hypothesis

Gravity changes how a gel seal forms, not how well a formed seal holds.

We expect pre-applied seals to perform the same in flight and on the ground, and live-applied seals to fail sooner in microgravity, because escaping blood stays trapped between the gel and the wound.

The set-up

A closed-loop circuit with four wound sites

The pump circulates blood simulant through a bypass for the whole flight. When microgravity is confirmed, a Mission Specialist diverts flow into one wound branch and the tourniquet valve opens over about five seconds. Flow meters before and after the wound give the leak rate.
